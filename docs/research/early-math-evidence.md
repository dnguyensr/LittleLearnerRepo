# Early Mathematics — Evidence Review

> **Reviewed:** 2026-09-05  
> **Scope:** A local, tap-first learning supplement for children from pre-K
> through early first grade. This review separates evidence about learning from
> standards that describe expected outcomes.

## Executive finding

Edamame already gives children useful experiences with one-to-one counting,
cardinality, order irrelevance, quantity conservation, subitizing, numeral and
quantity matching, addition/subtraction, number bonds, number lines, ten frames,
and base-ten representations. The interaction design is unusually careful about
recoverable errors and independent versus assisted work.

The largest educational gap is instructional coherence. Presentation renderers
often rotate without explicitly connecting their representations, and several
new concepts first appear as scored questions rather than modeled relationships.
The curriculum is also much deeper in arithmetic than in comparison, spatial
reasoning, shapes, measurement, and mathematical conversation.

The next Math work should therefore:

1. Define standards-neutral learning objectives and prerequisites.
2. Model each new relationship before independent practice.
3. Connect objects, pictures, language, and notation explicitly.
4. Add quantity comparison and mathematical language before more advanced
   arithmetic.
5. Treat short-run app performance as recent readiness, then confirm it later
   with changed examples or representations.
6. Add caregiver conversation and physical-world extensions.

## Evidence strength and applicability

### Teaching Math to Young Children

The 2013 IES/What Works Clearinghouse
[practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/18) covers preschool,
prekindergarten, and kindergarten. Its recommendation to teach number and
operations through a developmental progression has **moderate evidence**. Its
recommendations concerning geometry, patterns, measurement, data, progress
monitoring, mathematical talk in the world, and daily integration have
**minimal evidence** as isolated practices. Minimal does not mean disproven; it
means the panel relied more heavily on expert judgment and evidence from
multi-component interventions.

The developmental sequence includes recognizing small quantities, learning the
count sequence, pairing one word with one object, understanding that the final
word gives the set's quantity, comparing quantities, connecting quantities to
numerals, and then solving basic adding and subtracting situations. The guide
also recommends shape/space, pattern, measurement, and data experiences rather
than an arithmetic-only curriculum.

This guide is the primary source for Edamame's pre-K–kindergarten content order.
It is guidance for educator-led instruction, not evidence that an app produces
the same outcomes independently.

### Intentional instruction and mathematical conversation

The 2022 IES
[Preparing Young Children for School practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/30/i)
rates intentional instruction in mathematical ideas and skills as supported by
**strong evidence** and engaging children in conversations about mathematical
ideas and language as supported by **moderate evidence**.

This supports brief explicit models and caregiver-facing “Talk together” prompts.
It does not support forcing a verbal explanation from a child or using speech
recognition to judge mathematical understanding.

### Systematic instruction and representations

The 2021 IES
[elementary mathematics intervention guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/26)
gives strong-evidence recommendations for systematic instruction, clear
mathematical language, well-chosen concrete and semi-concrete representations,
and number lines. The included studies concern kindergarten through grade 6
students with or at risk for mathematics difficulty, generally receiving adult-
led supplemental instruction.

The guide supports Edamame's objects, ten frames, blocks, and number lines when
they function as thinking tools and are connected to mathematical notation. It
does not justify labeling a renderer “Common Core,” randomly displaying a
representation without explanation, or assuming independent app use is an
equivalent intervention.

### Spacing, retrieval, and connected representations

The IES
[Organizing Instruction and Study practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)
recommends spacing learning over time, interleaving worked examples with
problem-solving, combining graphics with verbal descriptions, and integrating
concrete with abstract representations. Its guidance applies from kindergarten
upward, though much of the underlying evidence is not specific to preschool or
touchscreen apps.

This supports a later-session readiness check and mixed review. It does not
prescribe Edamame's existing five-of-six threshold, a particular elapsed time,
or permanent mastery after six immediate responses.

### Preschool developmental expectations

The federal [Head Start preschool mathematics progression](https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/article/math-preschool)
includes pairing number words with objects up to at least 10, accurately
counting and comparing small groups with support, and duplicating simple
patterns. It is developmental guidance, not a controlled intervention study and
not a reason to treat all children of the same age identically.

### Adult-child use of digital media

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis reports a positive overall effect of
adult-child co-use on learning from digital media for children aged 0–6, while
also emphasizing variation among studies and the need for further research.
[Taylor and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100614)

Edamame should invite useful co-use without making solo use fail. A grown-up can
ask a follow-up question, offer household objects, or notice a strategy; the app
should not require the adult to operate controls for the child.

## Current product baseline

### Strong foundations already present

- Numbers coordinates a spoken count with one touched object at a time and then
  restates cardinality.
- A child may count objects in any order and may re-count the same quantity after
  rearrangement.
- Objects, dots, and a ten frame provide multiple representations of quantities
  zero through nine.
- Math separates problem generation from presentation renderers.
- Assisted and corrected completions celebrate without counting as independent
  readiness.
- Number bonds, ten frames, number lines, bar models, and base-ten blocks are
  interactive rather than static decoration.
- Subtraction and place value have short guided lesson sequences.

### Gaps and risks

- The Math foundation has no equivalent of the Words module's evidence review
  until this document.
- Counting and operations dominate. Direct comparison of sets/numerals,
  shape/space, and measurement are absent.
- First addition, counting on, decomposition, making ten, and fact relationships
  lack guided introductions.
- “Mix” rotates renderers but does not state how two representations encode the
  same relationship.
- Most arithmetic stories are result-unknown joining or separating. Unknown
  starts/changes, part-part-whole, and comparison structures are sparse or absent.
- Five correct responses in six immediate completions previously produced a
  permanent `mastered` flag. Math now treats that as recent readiness and pilots
  a later-session confirmation, while the legacy field name remains for storage
  compatibility.
- Arbitrary two-digit subtraction, carrying, and borrowing are available through
  parent-pinned settings without complete prerequisite lessons and extend beyond
  the product's early-first core.
- A single browser currently holds one learner's progress. Shared use invalidates
  readiness evidence.

## Standards compatibility, not standards-led design

Common Core remains useful as one quiet crosswalk because families and schools
may need to know whether an objective is familiar. It is not the organizing
curriculum and is never shown to a child.

[counting/cardinality standards](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/CC/)
and [operations standards](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/OA/)

experience but does not cover the full range, response form, problem structures,
or fluency expectation.

## Recommended objective order

This is a product proposal to validate, not a universal developmental claim:

1. Recognize quantities one through three without counting.
2. Coordinate one word with one object while counting to five.
3. State that the final number word tells how many.
4. Match quantities, spoken number words, and numerals zero through five.
5. Compare two small sets as more, fewer, or the same.
6. Extend the same relationships through ten with varied arrangements.
7. Compose and decompose quantities within five in more than one way.
8. Model joining and separating stories within five.
9. Count on from a known quantity and connect the action to addition notation.
10. Compose/decompose within ten and solve varied addition/subtraction stories.
11. Build ten and some more as the foundation for teen numbers.
12. Connect groups of ten and leftover ones to two-digit numerals.

Patterns and Shapes & Space run alongside this number progression rather than
waiting for arithmetic completion.

The repository-owned [early mathematics objective map](early-math-objectives.md)
turns this order into stable, standards-neutral ids with prerequisites,
instructional requirements, observable evidence, confirmation variation, and
secondary compatibility notes.

## Local observation questions

- Does the child know what action the prompt requests without an adult translating
  the interface?
- Does touching a manipulative support reasoning, or does the child discover a
  button sequence that bypasses the mathematical relationship?
- Can the child show the same quantity with different objects or arrangements?
- After seeing a pictorial or symbolic form, can the child represent it with
  physical objects?
- Does a later check reflect recall, or merely a repeated screen pattern?
- Which mathematical words does the child understand and use spontaneously?
- Do caregiver prompts create conversation without taking control away from the
  child?
- Does the child intentionally leave an activity that is too easy, too hard, or
  no longer interesting?

Record only a structured caregiver checklist. Add no analytics, recordings,
child names, or free-form identifying notes.

## Boundaries

- Evidence for educator-led or small-group instruction cannot be claimed as
  evidence for independent app effectiveness.
- Developmental sequences describe common paths, not fixed age deadlines.
- Automated correctness cannot establish mathematical explanation, strategy,
  fluency, retention, or real-world transfer by itself.
- Emoji and screen counters are representations, not substitutes for varied
  physical materials.
- Speech-off mode can remain operational, but it cannot provide equivalent
  mathematical-language instruction.
- Standards compatibility should remain partial and objective-specific until all
  required ranges, domains, and response forms are actually present.
